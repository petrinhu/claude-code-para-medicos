Title: Warfarin Versus Direct Oral Anticoagulants in Atrial Fibrillation: A Fictitious Comparison

Abstract:
This fictitious comparison contrasts a vitamin K antagonist with direct oral
anticoagulants for stroke prevention in atrial fibrillation. The simulated
results are invented for teaching and do not reflect any real trial.

Introduction:
Warfarin has a long history of use for stroke prevention in atrial fibrillation
but requires routine monitoring of the international normalized ratio and has
many food and drug interactions. Direct oral anticoagulants offer fixed dosing
and fewer interactions, at the cost of higher acquisition price in some settings.

Methods:
A fictitious population of simulated patients was followed for a simulated
period. Outcomes of interest were simulated stroke, simulated major bleeding,
and simulated all-cause mortality. No real data were used.

Results:
In this fictitious comparison, direct oral anticoagulants showed a simulated
stroke rate similar to warfarin and a lower simulated rate of intracranial
bleeding. Time in therapeutic range for the simulated warfarin group was modest,
which is a known limitation in everyday practice.

Dosing considerations:
Dose adjustment of direct oral anticoagulants may be required in patients with
reduced renal function, advanced age, or low body weight. Renal function should
be reassessed periodically, especially in older patients, because clearance can
decline over time and affect drug exposure.

Discussion:
Patient selection matters. Patients with mechanical valves or severe renal
impairment may still require a vitamin K antagonist. Shared decision making and
attention to adherence improve outcomes regardless of the agent chosen.

Conclusion:
In this fictitious comparison, direct oral anticoagulants offered a convenient
profile with a favorable simulated bleeding outcome. This text is for
demonstration purposes only.
